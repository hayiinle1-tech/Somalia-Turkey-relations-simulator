How the model works

Starting point. Every event carries a domain (security, energy, diplomacy, trade and aid, space, or Somali domestic politics) and an impact from −3 to +3. Impacts are summed with a 12-year fade and turned into a 0–100 score per domain. The relationship index is the weighted mean: security 30%, energy 22%, diplomacy 22%, trade and aid 16%, space 10%. Somali politics events set a separate legitimacy score: how much room Somali politics gives Turkey's role.

Scheduled forks. Each run first draws the Curad-1 well result (early 2027), then Somalia's planned 2027 elections, a possible second well in 2028 and Turkey's 2028 elections. Driver settings shift the odds of each branch.

Random shocks each quarter. Al-Shabaab offensives as AU troops withdraw, Turkish casualties, regional moves around Somaliland, Berbera and Ethiopia, spaceport milestones, armed political clashes, and parliamentary pushback when legitimacy is low.

Slow drifts. Alternative partners pull diplomacy and trade away. Turkey's fiscal capacity sets how much commitment Ankara can sustain, which feeds security and energy. Public backlash erodes legitimacy.

Scenarios. At the horizon each run is labelled by its final index and legitimacy: alliance (index 78 or higher), entrenched but contested (68 or higher with legitimacy under 42), steady (58 or higher), rebalancing (38 or higher), and drawdown or rupture (below 38).

Limits. Impact scores and weights are judgement calls, and the drivers are your assumptions. This tool explores how much the assumptions matter. It does not forecast. Treat the percentages as relative, and test them against the presets.

Refreshing the data. Run somalia_turkey_scraper.py on your computer. It collects new items from Google News feeds (including Turkish government sites and SONNA), the GDELT news database and the Turkish energy and defence ministries, tags each one by domain and impact, and writes events.json. Load that file above and the starting point recalculates.
